@@ 15-19
**ICAO's specimens**, printed in Doc 9303 for the fictional state of Utopia (`UTO`): a passport (TD3), and an ID card in each of the two card layouts (TD2 and TD1), for Anna Maria Eriksson, born 12 August 1974, documents expiring 15 April 2012. Because the standard prints them, they are an independent check: if our reading of the standard were wrong, they wouldn't pass.

@@ 22-26
**Check digits.** The standard's own worked example (520727 has check digit 3), the specimen passport's number, and an all-filler field (0).

@@ 29-35
**Every layout reads the specimens**: the right layout, type and issuer, the right dates, only the year and month of birth kept, and no repairs needed. `parametrize` runs the test once per specimen.

@@ 38-40
**Nothing identifying leaves the reader**: the result has exactly six fields, and none of them is a name, number or nationality.

@@ 43-47
**A tampered field is caught**: the year of birth changed from 74 to 75, its check digit left alone.

@@ 50-54
**A repair, only where it makes a check pass**: the OCR read a zero in the date of birth as the letter O. The reading is repaired, the right date comes out, and the repair is reported.

@@ 57-63
**The age rule.** The specimen expired in 2012, so today it fails as expired; on 1 January 2011 it passed. And the birthday arithmetic: 17 the day before an 18th birthday, 18 on the day, and a leap-day baby still 17 on 28 February of an ordinary year.

@@ 66-73
**Broken zones are refused with a reason**: too short to be any layout; a 33rd day of August, which its check digit catches before the date is even looked at; and a date with unknown parts.

@@ 74-77
One test per broken zone, each with its expected code.

@@ 80-86
**`compose_td3`** — a valid passport zone built the way an issuing state would build one: the fields padded with filler, and every check digit computed, including the composite over the right slices of the line.

@@ 89-92
What Hypothesis may invent: dates of birth from 1930 to 2025, expiry dates from 2027 to 2070, document numbers of 6 to 9 letters and digits, and personal numbers of up to 14 digits.

@@ 95-99
**Any valid zone reads back exactly**: Hypothesis builds a hundred random passports, and each must give back exactly the dates it was made with, without repairs.

@@ 102-110
**Any single-digit change to the date of birth is caught**: Hypothesis picks a date, a position and a new digit. If that isn't actually a change, there is nothing to test; otherwise the altered zone must be refused. This is the 7-3-1 rule's promise, tested rather than assumed.
